What it costs

Against
  • A floor takes money from households the draft deliberately charges nothing, to slow the bill for the highest earners. That is less progressive at both ends.
  • Households that pay nothing today, including many on the Oregon Health Plan, would pay more than today. So would the single public employee in the table, under Models 2 and 4.
  • Neither number is priced. The floor may not cover what the soft cap gives up.
  • The board left a soft cap out because it makes the structure less progressive, and its committee said avoidance "may be limited for most households."
For
  • Everyone above the poverty line pays something, and the working households in the examples still pay far less than today, with one exception.
  • A household at $2,000,000 still pays more than one at $1,000,000. It is not a cap.
  • A plan for everyone should not lean this hard on about 1 in 100 filers staying put.
  • The committee weighed a soft cap to avoid a schedule that "could be perceived as punitive relative to current health care payments."

What I would ask for: have the board's actuaries price a floor and a soft cap together, and show who pays more and who pays less, before the Legislature picks a model.
